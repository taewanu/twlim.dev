Sounds Abroad is a 3D globe for finding trending music by country. Pick a country and you get its current top tracks from Apple Music. Each track has a 30-second preview and links to Spotify and Apple Music.

Claude Code wrote most of the code. This page is about the parts it couldn't do.

## From idea to slice

Small slices keep the AI accurate. Give it a big, vague task and it returns something plausible and wrong. So I spent the most care on cutting scope.

I wrote a PRD first, to pin down why the app exists. It gave me a point to return to when the AI drifted.

Then I questioned every feature. Does it need to exist now? Does anything break without it? I cut until only the core was left.

Last, I split the work into thin vertical slices. I could check each one end to end before starting the next.

## One slice, one PR

One slice is one branch, and one branch is one PR. The AI can write across several slices at once, so I never bent that rule.

A pre-commit hook ran format, lint, and typecheck. Before pushing, I ran the same checks CI would run. The build catches what typecheck and lint miss, like failures at prerender or at an RSC boundary.

When a PR does one thing, I know where to look when something breaks. A small PR is also one I can read to the end, even when the AI wrote it.

## A second AI reviews

A second agent reviews the first agent's work and tries to break it. CodeRabbit reviews it too. Then I read all of it.

The reviewers are wrong sometimes. They also mix real defects with matters of taste. What to accept stays my call.

## The research I didn't accept

I wanted to translate song lyrics, so people could follow songs in other languages. An AI agent researched the licensing. Its answer was clear and well cited: not on my budget. A translation is a derivative work, so it needs the publisher's license.

I didn't accept it. Lyrics are all over the internet. How could the rules be that strict?

So I sent a second agent to try to overturn the first answer. The core held. I still couldn't host or translate lyrics. But it found a legal path the first had missed: link to the official lyrics page.

I cut translation. Instead, v1.2.0 added a short note on each song: what it's about and why it's charting there. A check blocks any note that quotes lyrics.

## What the AI got wrong

No compiler, test, or linter caught these.

The AI was sure the chart was server-rendered. Types, lint, tests, and build all passed. A controlled A/B showed it wasn't. The sheet sat inside a Radix Portal, which doesn't SSR.

Deferring the globe raised the LCP score. But no one got a better experience. So I refused it and redefined the gate around FCP, CLS, and TBT.

## Make it remember

A session's context is gone when it ends. So I keep decisions, dead ends, and lessons in memory files. The next session starts from what we already agreed.

A rule I wrote once still changes when a new case appears.

I write a short retro after every slice: what I did and what I learned. If I don't write it then, it's gone.

Bigger decisions go into ADRs as they happen. There are 22 so far.

## The gesture I replaced

V2 shipped on June 25. Free rotation was gone. A flick spun the globe and settled on a country. The goal was to make small countries easier to pick.

It didn't work. Neighbouring countries were still a few pixels apart under a fingertip.

<Clip src="/sounds-abroad/flick.mp4" poster="/sounds-abroad/flick.jpg" caption="The V2 flick, still live. Each flick lands on a new country." />

In July I wrote a new decision. You drag, and the country at the centre is highlighted. You release, and you land there. It's only partly shipped so far.

## Now

The app is live at [soundsabroad.app](https://soundsabroad.app). It covers 63 countries. Each country has one hidden gem: a local favourite, sometimes with a note on why it caught on.
