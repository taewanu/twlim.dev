When I travelled in Thailand, live gigs at restaurants and bars were a big part of the culture.

I've used Spotify for ten years. But I mostly open the app and play whatever it recommends. I rarely explore. Ironically, I didn't know what music was popular in the country I was in, or why.

So I thought of a fun way to explore what's trending around the world, like a drinking game.

Sounds Abroad is a 3D globe for finding trending music by country. Pick a country to see its top tracks on Apple Music. Each track has a 30-second preview and links to Spotify and Apple Music. I built it mostly with Claude Code.

## From idea to slice

At my previous company, our team tried to keep PRs as small as possible. Then Matt Pocock's [skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) introduced me to the term "vertical slice". It resonated, because that's what we had been aiming for.

I wrote a PRD first, to pin down why the app exists. Then I split the work into thin vertical slices.

## One slice, one PR

One slice is one branch, and one branch is one PR.

A pre-commit hook ran format, lint, and typecheck. Before pushing, I ran the same checks CI would run.

## A second AI reviews

A second agent and CodeRabbit review each PR.

## Make it remember

A session forgets everything when it ends. So decisions, dead ends, and lessons go into memory files, and each slice ends with a short retro.

Bigger decisions go into ADRs. There are 22 so far.

## The gesture I replaced

V2 shipped on June 25 and removed free rotation. A flick spun the globe and settled on a country. The goal was to make small countries easier to pick.

It didn't work. Neighbouring countries were still a few pixels apart under a fingertip.

<Clip src="/sounds-abroad/flick.mp4" poster="/sounds-abroad/flick.jpg" caption="The V2 flick, still live. Each flick lands on a new country." />

In July I chose a new gesture. You drag, and the country at the centre is highlighted. You release, and you land there. It's partly shipped.

## Now

The app is live at [soundsabroad.app](https://soundsabroad.app). It covers 63 countries. Each country has one hidden gem: a local favourite, sometimes with a note on why it caught on.
